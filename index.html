My name is Cheryl Parker, and I want to be your next city councilmember

Cheryl Parker has spent her career in public service, solving real infrastructure problems as a licensed Professional Civil Engineer. She knows how to ask the right questions, track the right numbers, and utilize her experience and skills to help Del Rey Oaks have projects delivered on time and on budget. Now she's bringing that same commitment to Del Rey Oaks, a city she calls home and wants to see thrive for generations to come.

Meet Cheryl

Putting Down Roots in Del Rey Oaks

Cheryl has called Del Rey Oaks home since 2020. Like many residents, she came here looking for a place to put down roots, and she found it. She and her husband have spent the past five years renovating their home in DRO and weaving themselves into the fabric of this community.

She grew up in a small rural town, where she learned early what it means to rely on your neighbors and to show up for them in return. Those lessons stayed with her — as did her grandmother, an immigrant from England, who taught her firsthand about the dignity of elder care, the realities of living on a fixed income, and what it looks like when a community either catches you or doesn't.

A Career Built on Accountability

Cheryl is a licensed Professional Engineer with a Master of Science in Civil and Environmental Engineering, graduating with distinction. She has worked as a transportation engineer, a private-sector consultant, a coal mining regulator, an environmental waste and drinking water engineer, and most recently as a Civil Engineer for the federal government and the Marina Coast Water District — working on the water supply and delivery infrastructure issues that matter most to Monterey Peninsula communities.

As a volunteer with Engineers Without Borders, she helped design and install a water pipeline for a rural village in Honduras — bringing a small community reliable water for the very first time.

A Critical Juncture for Del Rey Oaks

Del Rey Oaks is weighing major decisions on the development of its remaining land, working through serious infrastructure questions, and facing real financial pressure that could affect its future as an independent city. These aren't routine issues — they require careful analysis, technical judgment, and someone willing to ask hard questions before decisions are made.

"That's what she means when she talks about putting her knowledge and experience to work for the city: bringing technical expertise and a collaborative approach to the complex decisions ahead."

On The Issues

Cheryl's platform is built on the values of the residents — open process, real expertise, and a seat at the table for every neighbor

🏗️

Fort Ord Lands

Development that serves residents, present and future — with an open, competitive process and real public input before decisions are made.

Read more

Del Rey Oaks sits on the edge of one of the most significant land development opportunities on the Monterey Peninsula. How we develop former Fort Ord land will shape our city for generations — integrating the natural world into developed space, not paving over it.

  • An open, competitive process for any major development project
  • Prioritize development that serves residents, present and future
🔧

Infrastructure

18 years as a licensed P.E. designing fixes delivered on time, within budget, and with minimal impact on residents.

Read more

Infrastructure problems happen everywhere — and they require knowledgeable, experienced people to solve them. A licensed civil engineer on the council changes the dynamic: I know what questions to ask, and how to work through the answers.

  • Transparent oversight on all public infrastructure projects
  • Hold contractors to their commitments
📣

Public Voice

Every one of our 1,600 residents should feel their voice matters at city hall — before decisions are made, not after.

Read more

Real transparency means more than posting agendas: proactively sharing information, holding decisions open long enough for residents to respond, and ensuring public comment meaningfully shapes decisions. Cheryl also advocates restoring committees like Parks & Recreation, Arts, and Public Works.

  • Meaningful public comment before major decisions
  • Proactive communication on neighborhood projects
✈️

Airport Noise

The last MRY noise study was 2008. The taxiway is being moved right now. Those two facts don't add up.

Read more

Flight patterns, aircraft types, and a major taxiway relocation have all changed since 2008. Residents deserve to know whether that study still reflects reality.

  • Request an updated 14 CFR Part 150 Noise Exposure Map from MRY
  • Secure DRO representation in the noise abatement process
  • Establish a noise monitoring baseline in affected neighborhoods
🌱

Garden Center

A rare opportunity on prime city land. That decision belongs to residents — not whoever reaches the council first.

Read more

Cheryl supports a community-driven process to determine the best use of the former Garden Center property, with real public input before any commitments are made.

  • Community input before any use decision
  • No commitments without a public hearing
🤝

Community

A city our size punches above its weight when we build strong regional relationships — and below it when we don't show up.

Read more

We share infrastructure, services, and challenges with our neighbors on the Monterey Peninsula. On council, Cheryl will actively build those relationships and make sure DRO has a voice in regional decisions.

  • Active participation in regional planning bodies
  • DRO represented wherever regional decisions are made
🛡️

Public Safety

Support for our Public Safety Officers — and the sidewalks, crossings, and lighting that keep every resident safe.

Read more

Public safety is about more than policing — it's whether every resident, regardless of age or ability, can move safely through their own city. Cheryl supports an approach built on strong relationships between residents and the people who serve them.

  • Prioritize accessibility and safety improvements in city planning
  • Safety projects designed with input from the residents they serve
🌳

Preservation

Protecting the small-town character and green spaces we love — on purpose, not by luck — as the region grows around us.

Read more

Growth shouldn't come at the cost of what makes this city feel like home — a friendly, neighborly place close to the Frog Pond and the natural areas that surround us. That includes exploring a possible Frog Pond 2, a neighboring parcel that could become new accessible green space.

  • Protect green space and natural areas as DRO grows
  • Explore new green spaces like Frog Pond 2
